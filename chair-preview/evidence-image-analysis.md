# Chair reference analysis

## Suitability

- Verdict: conditional pass for a stylized real-time browser prop.
- Evidence: one product sheet contains a front view, a rear three-quarter view, a larger front three-quarter view, and dimensions.
- Limits: the underside, exact rear joinery, back-panel thickness, carving reverse faces, and hidden leg sockets remain approximate.

## Layered observations

1. Identification: a high-back upholstered dining/accent chair; object domain; confidence 0.96.
2. Silhouette: bilateral frame, narrow waist at the back cushion, arched crest, broad rounded seat, four long legs; proportions locked to 52 cm wide, 62.5 cm deep, 105 cm high and 48 cm seat height.
3. Macro assemblies: seat frame, seat cushion, back frame, back cushion, four-leg system.
4. Meso assemblies: carved crest, two open side frames, lower back rail, studded seat border, button grid, front/side/rear seat rails.
5. Micro features: six tuft buttons, shallow diamond tuft channels, perimeter nailheads, central crest rosette, mirrored scrolls, curved front-leg knees and feet.
6. Spatial relationships: cushion sits inside the wooden seat frame; back cushion is inset between side posts; crest attaches continuously to both posts; legs socket below the seat rails; rear legs align with the back posts.
7. Materials: deep brown stained wood, dielectric with satin response (roughness about 0.48); dark desaturated green leather, low metalness and medium roughness (about 0.42); antique brass nailheads, moderate metalness and roughness.
8. Identity features: arched floral crest, openwork side gaps, green button-tufted back, thick rounded seat cushion, nailhead border and visibly curved front legs.

## Approximation policy

- Hidden mortise-and-tenon joinery is represented as overlapping sockets.
- Rear upholstery backing follows the rear three-quarter photograph but its exact seam construction is approximate.
- Carving is modeled as layered procedural tubes, lobes and a rosette rather than manufacturing-accurate relief.
- Leather grain is represented by material response and shallow geometric seams; no photographic texture is projected.
